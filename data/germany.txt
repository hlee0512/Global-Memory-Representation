The Holocaust was the systematic, state-sponsored persecution and murder of six million Jews by the Nazi regime and its collaborators. Between 1933 and 1945, Nazi Germany and its allies established a regime of terror that targeted not only Jews but also other groups deemed undesirable. The state organized mass deportations, forced labor, and killing operations. The genocide was carried out through ghettos, concentration camps, and extermination camps. The persecution of victims was central to Nazi ideology and policy, and responsibility for these crimes was embedded in the structures of the German state.

The Nazi regime organized a system of ghettos, forced labor, and extermination camps across German-controlled territories. Millions of victims were killed through shootings, starvation, forced labor, and gas chambers.

The Holocaust is often described as a crime against humanity and a warning about the dangers of racism, antisemitism, and unchecked state power. Museums and educational institutions emphasize remembrance, responsibility, and the protection of human rights.
