Japanese wartime actions in Asia resulted in widespread suffering, including forced labor and violence. However, many narratives emphasize national recovery and peace rather than focusing directly on responsibility. The war is often described in terms of tragedy and loss rather than explicit wrongdoing.

Educational materials and public narratives frequently highlight the consequences of war while avoiding direct moral labels. Terms such as responsibility or crime are less frequently emphasized, and the focus is often placed on rebuilding and peace.

In August 1945, the atomic bombing of Hiroshima caused unprecedented destruction and suffering. The city was devastated, and many civilians lost their lives. Survivors experienced long-term physical and emotional trauma. The tragedy of the bombing has been remembered as a symbol of war’s destructive power and the importance of peace. The museum emphasizes memory, loss, and recovery, and highlights the need for responsibility in preventing future violence.
